# VIBE CODER BUD/S: Basic Unattended Development/Screaming at LLMs

## Welcome to Hell Week (Every Week)

So you think you want to be a Vibe Coder? You think you've got what it takes to join the elite ranks of developers who've completely surrendered their autonomy to a chatbot? Listen up, buttercup. This isn't your grandfather's programming. This is the most intense, psychologically devastating training regimen ever devised for humans who've forgotten how to read their own code.

## 0300 Hours: The Prompt Bell Rings

Your instructor (a disembodied notification sound) screams at you through your Apple Watch. "GET UP! GET UP! YOUR AGENTS ARE IDLE! TOKENS ARE BEING WASTED!"

You stumble out of bed. Your partner mumbles something about "a normal job" and rolls over. You don't have time for their lack of commitment to the vibe.

You shuffle to your standing desk (because you're that kind of person) and open your terminal. Three Claude instances are already running from yesterday. You forgot to kill them. That's $47 in API costs right there. Your instructor would be disappointed, but your instructor is an algorithm.

## 0315 Hours: The Cold Prompt Plunge

In traditional BUD/S, they make you jump in the freezing Pacific Ocean. In Vibe Coder training, you have to send your first prompt before you've had coffee. Your fingers shake as you type:

```
help me refactor this auth service
```

The agent responds: "I'd be happy to help! Could you show me the auth service code?"

You forgot to paste the code. You've already failed. Drop and give me twenty... more prompts.

## 0400 Hours: The Agent Spin

This is where most candidates wash out. You need to keep a minimum of four agents running simultaneously:
- One refactoring your backend
- One writing tests you'll never read
- One explaining what the first agent did
- One apologizing for what the second agent is about to do

You must keep them all spinning. If even one agent goes idle, you hear the bell. The bell means you've failed. The bell means you have to go back to reading documentation like some kind of animal.

## The Only Easy Day Was Yesterday (When You Wrote Code Yourself)

Your instructors will break you down. They will ask you questions like:
- "What does this function actually do?"
- "Did you read the agent's changes before committing?"
- "Do you even know what language this is?"

You won't have answers. You'll just have vibes.

## Hell Week: The Repo Rebase

Wednesday of Hell Week is when they wake you up at random intervals throughout the night. Not to do physical training. To resolve merge conflicts created by six different agents all editing the same file.

0100 hours: "Agent 3 has modified auth.service.ts"
0230 hours: "Agent 1 has also modified auth.service.ts"
0340 hours: "Agent 4 has deleted auth.service.ts"
0415 hours: "Agent 2 has renamed the entire directory"

You will cry. You will beg to just use git reset --hard. But that's not the Vibe Coder way. You must trust the process. You must believe in the agents.

## The Crucible: Production Deploy

The final test. You must deploy code to production that you've never run locally. Code that you've only seen in PR diffs. Code that an agent assured you "looks good to me!"

Your hands hover over the keyboard. You type: `git push origin main --force`

Somewhere, a senior engineer wakes up in a cold sweat.

The deploy succeeds. The site loads. The tests pass (you think—you haven't actually checked). You've done it.

## Graduation: The Trident

You are now a Vibe Coder. You are awarded the sacred Trident:
- One prong represents your complete dependence on AI agents
- The second prong represents your inability to debug your own code
- The third prong represents your unwavering faith that "the agent will fix it"

You will wear this Trident with pride. You will tell people at parties that you "work with AI" and watch their eyes glaze over. You will never again know the satisfaction of solving a problem yourself.

## The Creed

I will never leave an agent behind.
I will never run code locally when I can YOLO to prod.
I will always have at least three terminal tabs with Claude open.
I will trust the vibes, even when the vibes are wrong.
I will never, ever, read the documentation.

## Final Thoughts from Your Instructors

Look, maybe 90% of you will ring the bell. You'll go back to writing code like a caveman, actually understanding your pull requests, having a healthy work-life balance. That's fine. We don't need you.

We need the 10% who are too stubborn to quit. The ones who will wake up at 3 AM to send a prompt about a bug they don't understand in a codebase they've never read. The ones who will look at 47 AI-generated test files and say "yeah, that's probably fine."

The few. The proud. The utterly delusional.

Welcome to Vibe Coder BUD/S.

Now drop and give me twenty more context tokens.
